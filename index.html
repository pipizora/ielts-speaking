Part 1 大陆32主题逐题练习

01 Travelling

What transport do you often use when travelling?

I usually take a train between cities and use public transport once I arrive. I prefer keeping the journey fairly straightforward.

Do you often ride a bicycle?

Not very often. Walking and public transport usually fit my routine better, although I wouldn't mind cycling somewhere quiet.

Do you prefer to sit by the window when travelling?

Yes, if I have a choice. I like looking outside, and it makes the journey feel less monotonous.

Did you have a long journey with your family as a child?

I can't recall one particular long journey clearly. The childhood memories that come to mind more easily are playing with children who lived nearby.

02 Rubbish and recycling

Did you recycle when you were a kid?

Only in a simple way. We might keep cardboard or bottles, but I didn't think about recycling very systematically then.

Is waste sorting common in your country?

It seems fairly common where I live in Beijing. There are different bins, although how consistently people use them can vary.

How do you recycle things like paper and plastic?

I try to keep recyclable packaging separate from food waste. It helps if the packaging is reasonably clean.

What do you do when you see rubbish on the street?

If it's safe and easy to pick up, I'd put it in a bin. I wouldn't handle anything sharp or unpleasant with my bare hands.

03 Tiredness

What do you do when you feel tired?

I usually stay home and watch a series or some short videos. Sometimes I just need a quiet evening without making more plans.

When would you feel tired?

Usually after a demanding day at work or a long journey. Constantly having to concentrate can be as tiring as physical activity.

Do you often feel tired?

Fairly often when work gets busy. I enjoy the field, but the pace can be exhausting.

When you feel tired, do you still go out with your friends?

It depends on the plan. I'd still consider a relaxed meal with friends, but probably not something that involved a long journey.

04 Shoes

Which do you prefer, fashionable shoes or comfortable shoes?

Comfortable shoes, definitely. A pair can look great, but if my feet hurt after ten minutes, I won't want to wear them.

How much money do you usually spend on shoes?

I don't have a fixed budget. I care more about whether they're comfortable and likely to last than about the brand.

Have you ever bought shoes online?

Yes. It's convenient to compare styles and prices, although getting the right fit can be tricky.

Do you like buying shoes? How often?

Not particularly. I tend to buy them when I need a replacement rather than constantly looking for new pairs.

05 Politeness

What did you do to show your politeness as a child?

I was taught to greet adults and say thank you when someone helped me. Those small habits have stayed with me.

On what occasions should we be polite?

In everyday interactions, really. It matters especially when asking for help or disagreeing with someone.

Do you think being polite is very important?

Yes. Being polite makes communication easier, and it doesn't mean you have to agree with everything someone says.

How did you learn to be polite as a child?

Mostly from the adults around me. They reminded me what to say, and I also picked things up by watching them.

06 Fruit and vegetables

Do people in your country like planting vegetables?

Some do, especially if they have enough space. Someone I know grows spring onions because they're useful in everyday cooking.

Were there any kind of fruits and vegetables you disliked as a child?

I wasn't keen on bitter vegetables. I found the taste quite strong when I was little.

What kind of fruits and vegetables do you dislike?

I'm still not a big fan of very bitter flavours. But how something is cooked can make a difference.

Where do you usually buy fruit and vegetables?

Usually from a nearby shop or supermarket. Convenience matters when I'm only buying a few things for myself.

How often do you eat fruit and vegetables?

I try to include them regularly. Since I have a kitchen now, it's easier to prepare something at home.

07 Advertisement

Do you often see advertisements when you are on your phone or computer?

Yes, particularly on social media. Because I work in marketing, I sometimes notice how an ad is put together as well as what it's selling.

What kind of advertising do you like?

I like ads with a good joke or an unexpected story. The Karina and Faker Google Play commercial is one example I remember.

Do you like advertisements?

Some are entertaining, but repetitive ones can be annoying. I still judge a product by how useful it is to me.

Do you see a lot of advertising on trains or other transport?

Yes, I notice them around public transport. Large posters are hard to miss, even if I don't stop to read them.

Is there an advertisement that made an impression on you when you were a child?

No particular one comes to mind now. Recent ads are easier for me to remember, especially ones with a clear joke.

Have you ever bought something because of an advertisement?

An ad can make me look into a product, but I don't usually buy immediately. I'd want to check whether I actually need it.

08 Paper

Do you carry paper and pens with you when you go out?

Not usually. I tend to use my phone for quick notes, although a pen is useful when I know I'll need to write something down.

Do people still keep handwritten letters today?

Some people do, especially letters with sentimental value. A handwritten message can feel more personal than something on a screen.

Do you still write physical letters?

Hardly ever. Most of my communication happens online, so a handwritten letter would be for a special occasion.

What did you like to do with paper as a child?

I liked drawing. It was a simple way to keep myself entertained and explore characters I was interested in.

Have you made any crafts with paper?

Nothing particularly elaborate comes to mind. Drawing has been a bigger interest for me than making objects out of paper.

09 Secondary school

Is there anything you miss about your secondary school?

I miss seeing friends without having to arrange everything in advance. As adults, even finding time for dinner can take some planning.

What was your favourite subject at secondary school?

English was one of the subjects I was more comfortable with. I eventually studied it at university, although I wouldn't call it a lifelong passion.

Were there any subjects that you found difficult at secondary school?

Maths was difficult for me to enjoy. Later, that was one reason I decided international trade wasn't the right university major for me.

Do you remember your first day at secondary school?

Not in detail. I remember the general feeling of being somewhere new more than any particular event.

10 Name

Do you often forget people's names?

Sometimes, especially when I meet several people at once. I tend to remember the conversation better than the name.

How do you feel when people can't remember your name?

I don't take it personally. It happens to me too, so I'd just remind them.

How do you remember people's names?

I try to connect the name with something we talked about. Having a bit of context makes it easier to remember.

Is it easy for you to remember people's names?

Not always at first. Once I've spoken to someone a few times, it becomes much easier.

11 Opportunities

Should people try as many different things as they can?

Trying different things can help people discover what suits them. But they don't need to say yes to everything if it becomes exhausting.

Did you have any opportunities to take part in new activities as a child?

I had opportunities to try things informally, like drawing and playing games with other children. It wasn't always through organised classes.

12 Music

Do you prefer sad or happy music?

Both, depending on my mood. With musical theatre, I care more about how the song fits the story than whether it sounds happy or sad.

Does happy music make you feel more excited?

It can, especially when it's a song I already know. But I don't always want energetic music when I'm tired.

Have you taken any music classes?

Only ordinary school lessons, rather than serious musical training. Most of my interest comes from watching musicals.

Do you listen to music while doing other things?

Sometimes. It works well with routine tasks, but lyrics can distract me when I need to concentrate on language.

13 Teachers

Do you have a favorite teacher?

A teacher who explained things through examples is the kind I remember most. I found that much more useful than simply being told to memorise an answer.

Do you want to be a teacher in the future?

Probably not at the moment. I enjoy sharing useful information, but managing a whole classroom would be a different challenge.

Do you have a teacher from your past that you still remember?

Yes, I remember an English teacher who encouraged us to explain our opinions. That felt more engaging than only looking for the correct answer.

Are you still in touch with your primary school teachers?

No, not now. My family moved, and I've lost touch with quite a few people from my early school years.

In what way has your favourite teacher helped you?

The main help was making it easier to speak up. Having someone listen to the idea, rather than only the mistakes, made a difference.

Do you like your primary school teachers more than your high school teachers?

I wouldn't rank them as groups. Different teachers helped me at different stages.

14 Social media

Have you ever posted anything on social media?

Yes, I share bead patterns on Xiaohongshu. Some people have even made physical pieces from my designs, which is lovely to see.

When did you start using social media?

I don't remember the exact year. It's been part of how I keep up with friends and interests for quite a while.

Do you think you spend too much time on social media?

Sometimes. I might open an app for one video and stay much longer than I intended.

Do your friends use social media?

Yes. For example, I used to see my friend's travel updates when she was studying abroad.

What do people often do on social media?

They keep in touch, look for information and share their interests. I mainly notice theatre-related posts, artwork and everyday updates.

15 Tidiness

Do you like to keep things tidy?

Yes, within reason. I want my home to be comfortable and easy to use, rather than looking perfect all the time.

Did you keep your room tidy as a child?

Not consistently. It was easier to leave things where I'd been using them than to put everything away immediately.

How do you keep your work or study space tidy?

I try to keep the things I use most within reach and clear away things I no longer need. A simple system is easier to maintain.

Do you think that it is necessary to be tidy?

It helps, especially when you're trying to find something. But a lived-in home doesn't have to look like a showroom.

16 Websites

What kinds of websites do you often visit?

I use video and information sites, especially Bilibili. I watch entertainment, explanations and things connected with my hobbies.

What is your favourite website?

Bilibili is one I return to. I can find both theatre content and explanations of topics I know very little about.

Are there any changes to the websites you often visit?

I've noticed more recommendations competing for attention. It can make discovering things easy, but also makes it easy to keep scrolling.

What kinds of websites are popular in your country?

Video, shopping and social platforms seem popular among people I know. Different age groups have different favourites.

Do you prefer getting information from websites or books?

For a quick practical question, websites are more convenient. For something I want to explore in depth, a book can be easier to follow.

Would you like to have your own website?

Perhaps a simple page for my designs. It would be useful to have them in one place, although social media works for now.

What have you learned from websites that help with your life or studies?

I've picked up ideas for drawing and learned how to use tools. Online explanations have also helped me understand historical dramas.

17 Watch

Do you wear a watch?

Not regularly. I normally check the time on my phone, so a watch isn't essential for me.

Have you ever got a watch as a gift?

Not one that stands out in my memory. I'm more likely to remember gifts connected with my interests.

Why do some people wear expensive watches?

Some appreciate the design or craftsmanship, while others see them as status symbols. The reasons probably vary.

Do you think it is important to wear a watch? Why?

Not for everyone. It can be convenient, but there are plenty of other ways to check the time.

18 Shopping

Do you like shopping?

I enjoy it more as a relaxed activity with friends than as a reason to buy lots of things. Sometimes we just walk around a mall.

How often do you go shopping?

I buy everyday things when I need them. Trips to a shopping mall are more likely to be part of a weekend plan with friends.

Do you prefer online shopping or in-store shopping?

Online shopping is convenient for comparing products. But I'd rather try certain things, like shoes, in person.

Have you ever returned anything you bought online?

Yes, when something didn't fit properly. Clear return instructions make a big difference to the experience.

19 Cars

Did you enjoy traveling by car when you were a kid?

I enjoyed looking out of the window, although long journeys could get tiring. I wasn't particularly interested in the car itself.

What types of cars do you like?

Something practical and comfortable. I wouldn't choose a car simply because it looked expensive.

Do you prefer to be a driver or a passenger?

A passenger. I can relax and look around instead of having to concentrate on the road.

What do you usually do when there is a traffic jam?

If I'm a passenger, I might listen to something or check messages. The frustrating part is not knowing how long the delay will last.

Do you think car colours are important?

They matter to some people, but they'd be a secondary consideration for me. Practicality would come first.

Will you buy an expensive car in the future?

It's not a priority. I'd rather spend within my means than have a car that puts pressure on my budget.

20 Public gardens and parks

Did you like going to parks as a child?

Yes, mainly because I could play and spend time outside. I didn't think much about the scenery as a child.

Do you still like going to parks now?

Yes, especially when there is something different to experience. I once went to the Summer Palace in winter to try activities on the frozen lake.

Would you like to see more parks in your city?

Yes, particularly spaces people can reach easily from home. A famous park far away isn't a substitute for somewhere convenient to walk.

Are there any parks you want to go to in the future?

I'd be happy to revisit the Summer Palace in a different season. My winter visit was mostly about the ice activities.

Would you prefer to play in a personal garden or public garden？

A public garden suits me better. I can enjoy the space without having to maintain it myself.

How are the parks today different from those you visited as a kid?

Some seem to offer more organised activities and facilities now. But I wouldn't say every park has changed in the same way.

What do you like to do when visiting a park？

Usually walking and enjoying being outside. At the Summer Palace, my friend and I were much more focused on the ice bikes and sleds.

Would you like to play in a public garden or park？

Yes, if there's an activity I enjoy. It doesn't need to be competitive; simply doing something outdoors with a friend can be fun.

21 Science

Do you like science?

I'm interested in practical explanations, but I wouldn't call science my main interest. I like understanding things that affect everyday life.

When did you start to learn about science?

At school, though I can't remember the exact year. Much of what I learn now comes from things I happen to watch online.

Which science subject is interesting to you?

Biology can be interesting, especially when it helps explain animals. Having a cat gives me a reason to be curious.

What kinds of interesting things have you done with science?

I don't have a big scientific project to talk about. Most of my experience was through school lessons and simple activities.

Do you like watching science TV programs?

Occasionally, if the explanation is clear. I lose interest if it's just a long list of technical terms.

Do Chinese people often visit science museums?

Some families do, especially with children. I can't say how often people go overall, but they can be a useful outing.

22 Mirrors

Do you like looking at yourself in the mirror? How often?

Mostly when I'm getting ready to go out. It's practical rather than something I spend a lot of time doing.

Have you ever bought mirrors?

Just ordinary practical ones. I wouldn't usually treat a mirror as a special purchase.

Do you usually take a mirror with you？

Not usually. I don't feel I need a separate one with me all the time.

Would you use mirrors to decorate your room?

Possibly, if it suited the room. But I'd care more about making the space comfortable than creating a particular decorative effect.

23 Outer space and stars

Have you ever learnt about outer space and stars?

Only a little through school and general information. I know the basics, but I'm not someone who follows space news closely.

Do you like science fiction movies? Why?

Sometimes, if the characters and story are convincing. Impressive effects alone aren't enough to keep me interested.

Do you want to know more about outer space?

A little more, yes, especially explanations that don't assume specialist knowledge. It's an interesting subject even if it's not my main hobby.

Do you want to go into outer space in the future?

Probably not. The idea is fascinating, but I'd be happier learning about it from the ground.

24 Singing

Do you like singing? Why?

Casually, yes, especially if I know a song from a musical. I enjoy it without expecting to sound like a professional.

Have you ever learnt how to sing?

Not formally. Most of it is just following songs I like.

Who do you want to sing for?

Mostly myself, rather than an audience. Singing for other people would make me more self-conscious.

Do you think singing can bring happiness to people?

Yes. It can give people a way to express themselves, and singing together can make them feel connected.

Do you like listening to others singing?

Yes, particularly in musical theatre. The way a performer communicates a character matters to me as much as the voice.

Have you ever taken a singing class?

No formal classes. My interest has developed mainly as a listener and theatre-goer.

25 Clothing

What kind of clothes do you like to wear?

Comfortable clothes that are easy to put together. I like being able to go about my day without constantly adjusting what I'm wearing.

Do you prefer to wear comfortable and casual clothes or smart clothes?

Usually comfortable and casual clothes. I'd dress more carefully for a particular occasion, but comfort still matters.

Do you like wearing T-shirts?

Yes, they're easy to wear and combine with other things. They're useful when I don't want to spend much time choosing an outfit.

Do you spend a lot of time choosing clothes?

Not every day. I might think more about it before an outing, but I prefer a straightforward routine.

Do you wear different styles of clothes on weekdays and weekends?

Sometimes. A theatre visit or meeting friends might make me put a bit more thought into an outfit.

What colour clothes do you like?

I tend to prefer colours that are easy to match. I wouldn't say I have one favourite colour for every type of clothing.

26 Jokes & Comedies

Are you good at telling jokes?

I'm better at sharing a funny experience than delivering a prepared joke. Something that happened to my cat is easier for me to talk about naturally.

Do your friends like to tell jokes?

We joke around in conversation. It's usually about everyday situations rather than taking turns to tell formal jokes.

Do you like to watch comedies?

Yes, but the humour needs to feel natural. I enjoyed Qu Ying's relaxed conversation with Papi because she was funny without seeming to force it.

Have you ever watched a live show?

I've watched plays and musicals in person. If you mean stand-up comedy specifically, I don't have a clear example to give.

Are comedy shows popular in your country?

They seem to be popular online. People can share a short funny clip even if they haven't watched the whole programme.

27 Headphones

Do you use headphones?

Yes, they're useful when I want to watch something without disturbing other people.

What type of headphones do you use?

Usually wireless earphones. I find them convenient to carry around.

When would you use headphones?

When I'm watching videos or listening to something on public transport. At home, it depends on what I'm doing.

In what conditions would you not use headphones?

When someone is talking to me or when I need to pay close attention to my surroundings. I'd rather hear clearly in those situations.

Is wearing headphones comfortable?

For a while, yes, but I don't like wearing them continuously for hours. I prefer taking breaks.

28 Work or studies

What subjects are you studying?

I'm working now, but I studied English at university. Before that, I spent my first year studying international trade.

Do you like your subject?

I wasn't passionate about every part of it, but literature, interpreting and debate made it broader than school English.

Why did you choose to study that subject?

I switched because international trade didn't interest me, especially the maths. English seemed a better fit.

Do you think that your subject is popular in your country?

It's a familiar subject, although people have different reasons for choosing it. I chose it partly because it suited me better than my original course.

Do you have any plans for your studies in the next five years?

I don't have a fixed degree plan. I'd like to keep improving skills that are useful for my work and interests.

What are the benefits of being your age?

For me, having more independence is a benefit. I'm able to make more decisions about where I live and how I spend my money.

Do you want to change your major?

I've already graduated, but I did change majors at university. I moved from international trade to English after my first year.

Do you prefer to study in the mornings or in the afternoons?

For personal study, I prefer a time when I'm properly awake and not rushing. I don't have a strong preference that works every day.

How much time do you spend on your studies each week?

I'm not a full-time student now. The amount depends on what I'm learning and how busy work is.

Are you looking forward to working?

I'm already working. When I was a student, I was interested in becoming more independent, though work turned out to be quite demanding.

What technology do you use when you study?

I use online videos and digital notes. AI can help too, but I've learned that clear instructions make a difference.

What changes would you like to see in your school?

I've graduated, but more opportunities to apply what we learned would have been useful. Real communication isn't always like an exercise in class.

What work do you do?

I work in overseas marketing. Depending on the role, that can involve working with influencers or helping produce promotional content.

Why did you choose to do that type of work (or that job)?

It was related to my English degree, and I liked the international side of it. Reaching people in different markets seemed interesting.

Do you like your job?

I like learning about new ideas and trends. The downside is the workload and pressure in the internet industry.

What requirements did you need to meet to get your current job?

English communication and the ability to organise tasks were important. Some practical skills, like negotiating with influencers, I learned on the job.

Do you have any plans for your work in the next five years?

I'd like to keep developing in international marketing, but I also want a sustainable workload. I don't have every step planned.

What do you think is the most important at the moment?

Finding a balance between work and my own life. I want to keep learning without feeling that all my time belongs to work.

Do you want to change to another job?

I'm open to the right opportunity. I'd consider the work itself and the pace, rather than just the job title.

Do you miss being a student?

先直接回答，再选一个原因展开，最后用例子、比较或条件补充。通常先练出三到五句有内容的话；考官追问时继续展开，没有固定句数要求。问题里出现“所有人”“总是”“最重要”等词时，可以温和限定范围。

T01 决定与计划

观点方向： 小决定看效率，大决定看后果与可逆性；计划提供方向，但现实改变时应调整。个人例子：转专业、天黑后改拍摄地点。

表达： weigh up the options＝权衡选择；leave room for changes＝留出调整空间；the consequences are harder to reverse＝后果较难逆转

Which is better, to make a decision quickly or slowly?

It depends on what's at stake. For a small decision, spending too long can waste more time than it saves. But if the consequences are expensive or difficult to reverse, I'd rather compare the options carefully. Choosing lunch and choosing a university course don't need the same process.

What if people in a group have different opinions on a decision?

They should first agree on the goal and the constraints. That gives them a way to compare suggestions rather than just defend personal preferences. If time is limited, someone may need to make the final call, but people are more likely to accept it if they understand the reason.

How does technology help people make plans?

It makes information easier to gather and share. A group can compare routes, costs and availability without being in the same room. The limitation is that having more information doesn't automatically make the decision easier. People still need to decide what matters most and allow for changes.

What should people do to prepare for a job interview?

What should a leader do to make team members want to follow him or her?

A leader needs to make the goal clear and take people's input seriously. Team members are more willing to cooperate when they know why a task matters and how their work contributes. Being decisive helps, but constantly ignoring experienced colleagues can make decisions worse rather than stronger.

Do you think it is good to change jobs?

It can be, if the new role offers something important, such as better learning opportunities or a more sustainable pace. But changing jobs isn't automatically progress. I'd compare the actual responsibilities and working conditions, not only the title, and consider what I'd be giving up as well.

Learning about history, cultural traditions and everyday experiences in different regions can help. Shared identity doesn't have to mean that everyone lives in the same way. In fact, understanding the variety within a country can make that identity more thoughtful than simply repeating a few familiar symbols.

T08 城市建筑住房与噪声

观点方向： 地标观赏价值与日常便利分开；住房看交通、隐私、空间；行为要考虑场所而非禁止一切声音。

表达： everyday convenience＝日常便利；rely on lifts＝依赖电梯；suit your lifestyle＝适合生活方式

Do you think all old buildings should be demolished?

No. Some have historical or community value that a new building couldn't replace. At the same time, age alone doesn't make every structure worth preserving unchanged. I'd look at its condition, significance and possible future use, then consider whether restoration or adaptation would work.

Is it better to live in an apartment than in a house?

It depends on the person's priorities. An apartment may offer a convenient urban location with less space to maintain. A house may suit someone who values extra room. For me, access to transport and having privacy would matter more than the label attached to the building.

Should children not be allowed to make noise under any circumstances?

That would be unrealistic. Playing and expressing excitement naturally involve some noise. The expectation should depend on the place: a playground is different from a quiet library. Children can learn to consider others without being expected to remain silent everywhere.

T09 旅行交通与时间

观点方向： 计划减少浪费但不排满；旅行长度因目标而异；通勤同时集中造成压力；不要用个人体验推断所有人。

表达： a packed itinerary＝紧凑行程；get a feel for everyday life＝了解日常生活；make the most of time off＝利用假期

What is the ideal length for a holiday?

It depends on the distance and what the person wants from it. A short nearby break may be enough to relax, while a long journey needs more time to feel worthwhile. Personally, I prefer fewer places and more time in each, rather than spending most of the holiday in transit.

What problems does it cause for transportation when everyone commutes at the same time?

Demand becomes concentrated into a short period. Roads can become congested and public transport crowded, even if they work reasonably well at other times. Flexible start times might spread some of that demand, though not every job can offer them. Transport planning also has to consider where people live and work.

Is it good to arrive early in any situation?

Not necessarily. A flight or important appointment needs a buffer because delays have consequences. But arriving very early at someone's home may inconvenience the host. Being considerate means understanding the situation, not always treating the earliest arrival as the best one.

T10 艺术电影阅读与创造力

观点方向： 兴趣和表达有独立价值；作品帮助理解但可能刻板；创造力也包括修改和删减。

表达： one-dimensional characters＝单薄人物；visual storytelling＝视觉叙事；decide what to leave out＝决定删去什么

Does art, like music or theater, have an impact on society?

Yes, it can give people shared experiences and a way to discuss feelings or situations that are difficult to describe directly. A play may encourage a conversation long after it ends. That doesn't mean every work changes society dramatically, but small connections and new perspectives still matter.

Can movies help people better understand the cultural background of a country?

They can offer a starting point through settings, relationships and everyday details. But a film is one selected story, not a complete picture of a country. If viewers treat stereotypes as facts, they may misunderstand more than they learn. It helps to see different kinds of stories.

Do you think people should read the original book before watching the movie?

They don't have to. Reading first gives you your own image of the characters, but a film can also make someone curious enough to pick up the book. I'd see the two as different experiences. The important thing is not expecting every adaptation to work exactly like the original.

T11 朋友家庭代际与庆祝

观点方向： 关系靠共同时间与可靠支持；不同观念不必冲突；节庆让分散的人团聚，食物承载记忆。

表达： keep in touch＝保持联系；shared experiences＝共同经历；different priorities＝不同侧重点

Why do people need friends?

Friends can offer companionship and a different perspective from family or colleagues. Shared interests also make everyday life more enjoyable. You don't need a crisis for friendship to matter; having someone to discuss a play with or ask for a small favour can make a real difference.

Why is there a generation gap between the young and the old?

People grow up with different expectations and opportunities. What felt like a secure or successful choice to one generation may not fit another's situation. That can affect views on work or marriage. Understanding the background of an opinion doesn't require agreeing with it, but it can make the conversation less confrontational.

Why are there special foods on special occasions or events?

Food can connect an occasion with memory and family routines. Preparing something familiar makes the event feel different from an ordinary day. The dish doesn't have to be expensive; sometimes its value comes from who makes it and the associations people have built around it over many years.

T12 健康运动与生活节奏

观点方向： 运动不只竞技；小习惯容易坚持；讨论压力不做个体诊断；看体育可因集体归属而非专业兴趣。

表达： fit movement into the day＝在一天中加入活动；a sense of belonging＝归属感；a sustainable routine＝可持续习惯

Why do some people like to watch sports events?

Some enjoy the skill and uncertainty of the result. Others care about supporting a team or sharing the atmosphere with people around them. I experienced that at a university basketball game. I wasn't a sports expert, but having my own university involved gave me a reason to pay attention.

What effect does stress have on health?

Sustained stress can make it harder for people to rest and maintain ordinary routines. Someone under pressure may feel they have little time for sleep or exercise. I wouldn't assume everyone reacts in the same way, but a manageable workload and opportunities to recover seem important.

Why do some people like to stay up late?

For some working adults, it's the only time that feels fully their own. After a demanding day, going straight to bed can feel like giving up that personal time. That helps explain the habit even if it makes the next day harder. Changing it may involve changing the evening routine, not just setting an alarm.

T13 自然植物环境与规则

观点方向： 环境影响生活条件；方便的设施帮助遵守规则；规定需解释和一致执行；环保提议与法律事实分开。

表达： make the right choice easier＝让合适选择更容易；clear and practical rules＝清晰可行的规则；shared resources＝共同资源

Which is more important, protecting the environment or developing the economy?

I don't think they're completely separate. Economic activity depends on conditions people can live and work in, while environmental projects need resources. The difficult question is how to handle short-term costs. I'd favour development that accounts for those costs rather than treating environmental damage as someone else's problem.

Why do some people like to grow plants?

Some enjoy seeing something develop through their care. Others like having greenery or herbs they can use in cooking. It doesn't have to be a demanding hobby; choosing suitable plants can make it a small part of daily life. The appeal may be practical, emotional or both.

How can parents and teachers help children understand and follow rules?

They should explain the purpose and apply the rule consistently. A child is more likely to understand a quiet area if they can see that others are trying to concentrate. Adults also need to model the behaviour. A rule feels less convincing when the people explaining it ignore it themselves.

T14 动物照顾与帮助他人

观点方向： 宠物提供陪伴但有照顾责任；城市养宠考虑空间及他人；帮助重在具体需求和尊重。

表达： provide companionship＝提供陪伴；someone reliable＝可靠的人；a practical favour＝实际的小忙

Many people regard pets as members of their family. What do you think?

I can understand that. Caring for an animal becomes part of someone's daily routine, and the companionship can be important, especially for a person living alone. That affection also comes with responsibility. Seeing a pet as family should include meeting its needs, not only enjoying the comforting moments.

Some people think pets should not be kept in cities. What do you think?

A blanket ban seems too broad. What matters is whether owners can meet the animal's needs and avoid causing problems for neighbours or shared spaces. Different animals need different conditions. Responsible care is a more useful focus than assuming that every city home is unsuitable.

Should children be taught to be kind to others?

Yes, through practical actions they can understand. Helping with a small task or noticing when someone needs support gives kindness a concrete meaning. I'd also teach them to respect what the other person actually wants, because helping isn't only about feeling good about your own intentions.
