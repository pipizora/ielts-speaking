Part 1 大陆32主题逐题练习

01 Travelling

What transport do you often use when travelling?

Usually trains between cities, and then the metro or buses once I get there. I like knowing roughly how long the journey will take, without having to worry about driving or parking.

Do you often ride a bicycle?

Not very often. I mostly walk or take public transport. I'd be more interested in cycling somewhere quiet than trying to ride through busy traffic.

Do you prefer to sit by the window when travelling?

Yes, definitely. I like having something to look at, especially on a train. Even if the journey's long, watching the scenery makes it feel less boring.

Did you have a long journey with your family as a child?

I don't remember a particular long trip, to be honest. My clearest childhood memories are of playing with children who lived nearby, rather than travelling far from home.

02 Rubbish and recycling

Did you recycle when you were a kid?

A little. We sometimes kept things like cardboard and plastic bottles instead of throwing them away with everything else. I didn't really understand much about recycling at the time, though.

Is waste sorting common in your country?

It's common in many cities, including Beijing, where I live. We have separate bins for different kinds of rubbish, although people don't always sort everything correctly.

How do you recycle things like paper and plastic?

I keep paper and plastic packaging separate from food waste and put them in the recycling bin. I also try to empty the packaging first, rather than throw it in with food still inside.

What do you do when you see rubbish on the street?

If it's something like a clean piece of paper, I'd pick it up and find a bin. But I wouldn't touch broken glass or anything that could hurt me.

03 Tiredness

What do you do when you feel tired?

I usually stay home and watch a series or a few videos. If I've had a really busy day, I just want to sit down and not have to think about anything complicated.

When would you feel tired?

Usually after a busy day at work. Having to concentrate for hours and deal with lots of little problems can really wear me out, even if I've been sitting down most of the time.

Do you often feel tired?

Quite often when work gets busy. I like working in overseas marketing, but the pace and pressure can be a lot. By the evening, I sometimes have very little energy left.

When you feel tired, do you still go out with your friends?

Sometimes, if it's just dinner somewhere nearby. Seeing friends can help me relax. But if the plan involves travelling across the city, I'd probably leave it for another day.

04 Shoes

Which do you prefer, fashionable shoes or comfortable shoes?

Comfortable ones, definitely. I do a fair amount of walking, so there's no point in buying a beautiful pair that hurts my feet after ten minutes.

How much money do you usually spend on shoes?

I don't keep track of the exact amount, but I'd be comfortable spending a few hundred yuan on a pair I could wear often. I care more about comfort and how long they'll last than the logo.

Have you ever bought shoes online?

Yes, I have. It's easy to compare prices online, but the fit can be a problem. I pay attention to the size information and whether I can return them.

Do you like buying shoes? How often?

Not especially. I only look for a new pair when I need one, rather than browsing every month. Usually it's because an old pair is wearing out.

05 Politeness

What did you do to show your politeness as a child?

I greeted adults and said thank you when someone helped me or gave me something. They were small things, but my family would remind me if I forgot.

On what occasions should we be polite?

In most everyday situations, really, especially when we're asking for help. I also think it matters when we disagree with someone, because it's easy to sound rude without meaning to.

Do you think being polite is very important?

Yes. People are much more willing to help if you speak to them nicely. It doesn't mean agreeing with everyone; you can say no and still be polite.

How did you learn to be polite as a child?

Mostly from my family. They reminded me to say things like 'please' and 'thank you', and I picked up a lot just by watching how they spoke to other people.

06 Fruit and vegetables

Do people in your country like planting vegetables?

Quite a few do, if they have the space. My classmate's mother grows things like spring onions. I can see the appeal: you can just pick a little when you're cooking.

Were there any kind of fruits and vegetables you disliked as a child?

I didn't like bitter vegetables very much. The taste was too strong for me as a child, so I'd usually try to avoid them if I could.

What kind of fruits and vegetables do you dislike?

I'm still not keen on very bitter vegetables. It depends how they're cooked, though. Sometimes a different way of preparing something makes it much nicer.

Where do you usually buy fruit and vegetables?

Usually at a nearby shop or supermarket. I live alone, so I tend to buy small amounts. I don't want half of it going bad before I can eat it.

How often do you eat fruit and vegetables?

On most days, although I could probably eat a wider variety. Having my own kitchen helps, because it's easier to add some vegetables when I cook for myself.

07 Advertisement

Do you often see advertisements when you are on your phone or computer?

Yes, all the time, especially when I'm scrolling through social media. Since I work in marketing, I sometimes pay attention to the way an ad is made as well as the product.

What kind of advertising do you like?

I like funny ads with an unexpected ending. The Google Play ad with Karina and Faker comes to mind: you're expecting romance, but he seems far more interested in the game.

Do you like advertisements?

Some of them. A good one can be entertaining, but seeing the same ad over and over gets annoying. I don't enjoy being interrupted every time I want to watch something.

Do you see a lot of advertising on trains or other transport?

Yes, especially around the metro. There are big posters and screens, so they're hard to miss even when I'm not really paying attention.

Is there an advertisement that made an impression on you when you were a child?

Nothing specific comes to mind now. I can remember recent funny ads much more easily than anything I saw as a child.

Have you ever bought something because of an advertisement?

I can't think of a clear example off the top of my head. An ad might make me look at a product, but I usually check whether I need it and compare options before buying.

08 Paper

Do you carry paper and pens with you when you go out?

Not usually, no. I use my phone for notes, so I don't normally carry a notebook or loose paper. I'd take a pen if I knew I had a form to fill in.

Do people still keep handwritten letters today?

Some people do, especially if the letter is from someone important to them. It's something you can keep and look back at, and the handwriting makes it feel personal.

Do you still write physical letters?

Hardly ever. I mostly send messages on my phone. A handwritten letter would be something I'd save for a special occasion, rather than everyday communication.

What did you like to do with paper as a child?

I liked drawing on it. I could spend time copying characters or trying out my own ideas, and all I needed was some paper and a pencil.

Have you made any crafts with paper?

I can't remember making anything elaborate. Drawing was more my thing, so I was more likely to cover the paper with pictures than turn it into an object.

09 Secondary school

Is there anything you miss about your secondary school?

I miss seeing my friends every day without having to arrange anything. Now we're working, even getting everyone together for dinner can take quite a bit of planning.

What was your favourite subject at secondary school?

I'd probably say English. I found it more interesting than maths, although I wouldn't say I was passionate about every lesson. Later, I ended up studying it at university.

Were there any subjects that you found difficult at secondary school?

Maths, definitely. I found it hard to follow and didn't enjoy it much, which made studying it even harder. It was a relief to move towards a subject that suited me better.

Do you remember your first day at secondary school?

Not very clearly. I remember the feeling of being somewhere unfamiliar more than a particular thing that happened. The people and everyday routines are what I remember best now.

10 Name

Do you often forget people's names?

Sometimes, especially if I'm introduced to several people at once. I might remember what we talked about but completely forget the name, which can be a bit awkward.

How do you feel when people can't remember your name?

I don't mind much. It happens to me too, so I'd just tell them again. I'd be more bothered if someone was rude than if they simply forgot my name.

How do you remember people's names?

I try to link the name to something about the person, like where we met or what we talked about. That gives me something more to remember than just the name on its own.

Is it easy for you to remember people's names?

Not straight away. Once I've spoken to someone a few times, it's much easier. Meeting a lot of new people in one go is when I struggle.

11 Opportunities

Should people try as many different things as they can?

Yes, within reason. Trying something is often the only way to find out whether you enjoy it. But I wouldn't turn every free hour into another activity; having time to relax matters too.

Did you have any opportunities to take part in new activities as a child?

Yes, mostly simple things like drawing and games with other children. We lived close to each other, so we could try things together without our parents having to organise a special trip.

12 Music

Do you prefer sad or happy music?

Both, depending on my mood. With musical theatre, I care more about how the song fits the story than whether it sounds happy or sad.

Does happy music make you feel more excited?

It can, especially when it's a song I already know. But I don't always want energetic music when I'm tired.

Have you taken any music classes?

Only ordinary school lessons, rather than serious musical training. Most of my interest comes from watching musicals.

Do you listen to music while doing other things?

Sometimes. It works well with routine tasks, but lyrics can distract me when I need to concentrate on language.

13 Teachers

Do you have a favorite teacher?

A teacher who explained things through examples is the kind I remember most. I found that much more useful than simply being told to memorise an answer.

Do you want to be a teacher in the future?

Probably not at the moment. I enjoy sharing useful information, but managing a whole classroom would be a different challenge.

Do you have a teacher from your past that you still remember?

Yes, I remember an English teacher who encouraged us to explain our opinions. That felt more engaging than only looking for the correct answer.

Are you still in touch with your primary school teachers?

No, not now. My family moved, and I've lost touch with quite a few people from my early school years.

In what way has your favourite teacher helped you?

The main help was making it easier to speak up. Having someone listen to the idea, rather than only the mistakes, made a difference.

Do you like your primary school teachers more than your high school teachers?

I wouldn't rank them as groups. Different teachers helped me at different stages.

14 Social media

Have you ever posted anything on social media?

Yes, I share bead patterns on Xiaohongshu. Some people have even made physical pieces from my designs, which is lovely to see.

When did you start using social media?

I don't remember the exact year. It's been part of how I keep up with friends and interests for quite a while.

Do you think you spend too much time on social media?

Sometimes. I might open an app for one video and stay much longer than I intended.

Do your friends use social media?

Yes. For example, I used to see my friend's travel updates when she was studying abroad.

What do people often do on social media?

They keep in touch, look for information and share their interests. I mainly notice theatre-related posts, artwork and everyday updates.

15 Tidiness

Do you like to keep things tidy?

Yes, within reason. I want my home to be comfortable and easy to use, rather than looking perfect all the time.

Did you keep your room tidy as a child?

Not consistently. It was easier to leave things where I'd been using them than to put everything away immediately.

How do you keep your work or study space tidy?

I try to keep the things I use most within reach and clear away things I no longer need. A simple system is easier to maintain.

Do you think that it is necessary to be tidy?

It helps, especially when you're trying to find something. But a lived-in home doesn't have to look like a showroom.

16 Websites

What kinds of websites do you often visit?

I use video and information sites, especially Bilibili. I watch entertainment, explanations and things connected with my hobbies.

What is your favourite website?

Bilibili is one I return to. I can find both theatre content and explanations of topics I know very little about.

Are there any changes to the websites you often visit?

I've noticed more recommendations competing for attention. It can make discovering things easy, but also makes it easy to keep scrolling.

What kinds of websites are popular in your country?

Video, shopping and social platforms seem popular among people I know. Different age groups have different favourites.

Do you prefer getting information from websites or books?

For a quick practical question, websites are more convenient. For something I want to explore in depth, a book can be easier to follow.

Would you like to have your own website?

Perhaps a simple page for my designs. It would be useful to have them in one place, although social media works for now.

What have you learned from websites that help with your life or studies?

I've picked up ideas for drawing and learned how to use tools. Online explanations have also helped me understand historical dramas.

17 Watch

Do you wear a watch?

Not regularly. I normally check the time on my phone, so a watch isn't essential for me.

Have you ever got a watch as a gift?

Not one that stands out in my memory. I'm more likely to remember gifts connected with my interests.

Why do some people wear expensive watches?

Some appreciate the design or craftsmanship, while others see them as status symbols. The reasons probably vary.

Do you think it is important to wear a watch? Why?

Not for everyone. It can be convenient, but there are plenty of other ways to check the time.

18 Shopping

Do you like shopping?

I enjoy it more as a relaxed activity with friends than as a reason to buy lots of things. Sometimes we just walk around a mall.

How often do you go shopping?

I buy everyday things when I need them. Trips to a shopping mall are more likely to be part of a weekend plan with friends.

Do you prefer online shopping or in-store shopping?

Online shopping is convenient for comparing products. But I'd rather try certain things, like shoes, in person.

Have you ever returned anything you bought online?

Yes, when something didn't fit properly. Clear return instructions make a big difference to the experience.

19 Cars

Did you enjoy traveling by car when you were a kid?

I enjoyed looking out of the window, although long journeys could get tiring. I wasn't particularly interested in the car itself.

What types of cars do you like?

Something practical and comfortable. I wouldn't choose a car simply because it looked expensive.

Do you prefer to be a driver or a passenger?

A passenger. I can relax and look around instead of having to concentrate on the road.

What do you usually do when there is a traffic jam?

If I'm a passenger, I might listen to something or check messages. The frustrating part is not knowing how long the delay will last.

Do you think car colours are important?

They matter to some people, but they'd be a secondary consideration for me. Practicality would come first.

Will you buy an expensive car in the future?

It's not a priority. I'd rather spend within my means than have a car that puts pressure on my budget.

20 Public gardens and parks

Did you like going to parks as a child?

Yes, mainly because I could play and spend time outside. I didn't think much about the scenery as a child.

Do you still like going to parks now?

Yes, especially when there is something different to experience. I once went to the Summer Palace in winter to try activities on the frozen lake.

Would you like to see more parks in your city?

Yes, particularly spaces people can reach easily from home. A famous park far away isn't a substitute for somewhere convenient to walk.

Are there any parks you want to go to in the future?

I'd be happy to revisit the Summer Palace in a different season. My winter visit was mostly about the ice activities.

Would you prefer to play in a personal garden or public garden？

A public garden suits me better. I can enjoy the space without having to maintain it myself.

How are the parks today different from those you visited as a kid?

Some seem to offer more organised activities and facilities now. But I wouldn't say every park has changed in the same way.

What do you like to do when visiting a park？

Usually walking and enjoying being outside. At the Summer Palace, my friend and I were much more focused on the ice bikes and sleds.

Would you like to play in a public garden or park？

Yes, if there's an activity I enjoy. It doesn't need to be competitive; simply doing something outdoors with a friend can be fun.

21 Science

Do you like science?

I'm interested in practical explanations, but I wouldn't call science my main interest. I like understanding things that affect everyday life.

When did you start to learn about science?

At school, though I can't remember the exact year. Much of what I learn now comes from things I happen to watch online.

Which science subject is interesting to you?

Biology can be interesting, especially when it helps explain animals. Having a cat gives me a reason to be curious.

What kinds of interesting things have you done with science?

I don't have a big scientific project to talk about. Most of my experience was through school lessons and simple activities.

Do you like watching science TV programs?

Occasionally, if the explanation is clear. I lose interest if it's just a long list of technical terms.

Do Chinese people often visit science museums?

Some families do, especially with children. I can't say how often people go overall, but they can be a useful outing.

22 Mirrors

Do you like looking at yourself in the mirror? How often?

Mostly when I'm getting ready to go out. It's practical rather than something I spend a lot of time doing.

Have you ever bought mirrors?

Just ordinary practical ones. I wouldn't usually treat a mirror as a special purchase.

Do you usually take a mirror with you？

Not usually. I don't feel I need a separate one with me all the time.

Would you use mirrors to decorate your room?

Possibly, if it suited the room. But I'd care more about making the space comfortable than creating a particular decorative effect.

23 Outer space and stars

Have you ever learnt about outer space and stars?

Only a little through school and general information. I know the basics, but I'm not someone who follows space news closely.

Do you like science fiction movies? Why?

Sometimes, if the characters and story are convincing. Impressive effects alone aren't enough to keep me interested.

Do you want to know more about outer space?

A little more, yes, especially explanations that don't assume specialist knowledge. It's an interesting subject even if it's not my main hobby.

Do you want to go into outer space in the future?

Probably not. The idea is fascinating, but I'd be happier learning about it from the ground.

24 Singing

Do you like singing? Why?

Casually, yes, especially if I know a song from a musical. I enjoy it without expecting to sound like a professional.

Have you ever learnt how to sing?

Not formally. Most of it is just following songs I like.

Who do you want to sing for?

Mostly myself, rather than an audience. Singing for other people would make me more self-conscious.

Do you think singing can bring happiness to people?

Yes. It can give people a way to express themselves, and singing together can make them feel connected.

Do you like listening to others singing?

Yes, particularly in musical theatre. The way a performer communicates a character matters to me as much as the voice.

Have you ever taken a singing class?

No formal classes. My interest has developed mainly as a listener and theatre-goer.

25 Clothing

What kind of clothes do you like to wear?

Comfortable clothes that are easy to put together. I like being able to go about my day without constantly adjusting what I'm wearing.

Do you prefer to wear comfortable and casual clothes or smart clothes?

Usually comfortable and casual clothes. I'd dress more carefully for a particular occasion, but comfort still matters.

Do you like wearing T-shirts?

Yes, they're easy to wear and combine with other things. They're useful when I don't want to spend much time choosing an outfit.

Do you spend a lot of time choosing clothes?

Not every day. I might think more about it before an outing, but I prefer a straightforward routine.

Do you wear different styles of clothes on weekdays and weekends?

Sometimes. A theatre visit or meeting friends might make me put a bit more thought into an outfit.

What colour clothes do you like?

I tend to prefer colours that are easy to match. I wouldn't say I have one favourite colour for every type of clothing.

26 Jokes & Comedies

Are you good at telling jokes?

I'm better at sharing a funny experience than delivering a prepared joke. Something that happened to my cat is easier for me to talk about naturally.

Do your friends like to tell jokes?

We joke around in conversation. It's usually about everyday situations rather than taking turns to tell formal jokes.

Do you like to watch comedies?

Yes, but the humour needs to feel natural. I enjoyed Qu Ying's relaxed conversation with Papi because she was funny without seeming to force it.

Have you ever watched a live show?

I've watched plays and musicals in person. If you mean stand-up comedy specifically, I don't have a clear example to give.

Are comedy shows popular in your country?

27 Headphones

Yes, they're useful when I want to watch something without disturbing other people.

What type of headphones do you use?

Usually wireless earphones. I find them convenient to carry around.

When I'm watching videos or listening to something on public transport. At home, it depends on what I'm doing.

When someone is talking to me or when I need to pay close attention to my surroundings. I'd rather hear clearly in those situations.

For a while, yes, but I don't like wearing them continuously for hours. I prefer taking breaks.

28 Work or studies

What subjects are you studying?

I'm working now, but I studied English at university. Before that, I spent my first year studying international trade.

Do you like your subject?

I wasn't passionate about every part of it, but literature, interpreting and debate made it broader than school English.

I don't have a fixed degree plan. I'd like to keep improving skills that are useful for my work and interests.

T01 决定与计划

观点方向： 小决定看效率，大决定看后果与可逆性；计划提供方向，但现实改变时应调整。个人例子：转专业、天黑后改拍摄地点。

表达： weigh up the options＝权衡选择；leave room for changes＝留出调整空间；the consequences are harder to reverse＝后果较难逆转

Which is better, to make a decision quickly or slowly?

For small things, quickly is usually fine. You don't need half an hour to decide what to have for lunch. But if you're choosing a university course or changing jobs, it's worth taking more time. You want to know what you're getting into before you make a decision that's hard to undo.

What if people in a group have different opinions on a decision?

I'd start by asking what everyone wants to achieve. Sometimes people disagree because they're worrying about different things: one person cares about the price, while another cares about saving time. Once that's clear, it's easier to find a compromise. If there's a deadline, the group also needs to agree who makes the final decision.

How does technology help people make plans?

It makes the practical side much easier. If you're planning a trip with friends, you can compare hotels, check routes and share everything in a group chat. You don't all have to meet in person. The trouble is that you can spend ages comparing options, so at some point you still need to choose one.

T02 工作团队与职业发展

观点方向： 成长、收入与可持续工作量一起看；团队靠分工和沟通；面试要看具体能力，不等于只看口才。

表达： a manageable workload＝可承受的工作量；bring different skills＝带来不同技能；give people clear expectations＝让人清楚要求

What should people do to prepare for a job interview?

They should find out what the job actually involves and prepare a few examples from their own experience. For instance, if the job needs teamwork, they could explain a project they worked on and what they did. That's more convincing than just saying, 'I'm a team player.' I'd also prepare some questions about the role.

What should a leader do to make team members want to follow him or her?

A good leader should make it clear what needs doing and listen when people raise a problem. If someone has more experience with a task, it makes sense to hear their suggestion. People also notice whether the leader helps when things go wrong. It's much easier to follow someone who takes responsibility instead of just blaming the team.

Do you think it is good to change jobs?

It can be a good move if the new job gives you something you're missing, like better pay or a chance to learn. But I'd check what the daily work is actually like. A more impressive title doesn't help much if you're constantly exhausted. So I think the reason for changing matters more than the change itself.

T03 商业消费与金钱

观点方向： 小生意成功可指覆盖生活开支、稳定经营；消费者看实用与预算；省钱有时牺牲便利。

表达： cover everyday expenses＝覆盖日常开支；value for money＝性价比；within your means＝在经济能力范围内

Does money equal success?

No, although having enough money certainly makes life easier. My parents run a small food stall, and for them, covering their living costs is a good result. They don't need to turn it into a huge company. Someone else might aim much higher, so I think success depends partly on what you're trying to achieve.

Should schools teach children how to manage money from a young age?

Yes, but I'd keep it practical. Give a child a small amount to spend and let them decide whether to buy something now or save for something they want more. They'll understand the trade-off much better that way. You don't need complicated lessons about finance to teach them that money runs out.

What are the advantages and disadvantages of shopping online?

The biggest advantage is convenience. You can compare lots of products without visiting several shops. The downside is that you can't try them first: shoes might look great online and still hurt when you put them on. That's why I check the return policy, especially for something where the size or fit matters.

T04 教育技能与语言

观点方向： 练习提供反馈，老师帮助定位问题；自学灵活但需判断信息；孩子与成人各有优势，不一概而论。

表达： learn through trial and error＝尝试纠错；get specific feedback＝获得具体反馈；put knowledge into practice＝应用知识

What are the differences between learning from a teacher and learning by oneself?

A teacher can tell you what's going wrong before you spend weeks practising the wrong thing. That's really useful when you're starting out. Learning by yourself is more flexible, though, and you can focus on what interests you. I'd probably combine them: try it myself first, then ask someone when I get stuck.

Do you think it is easier for children to learn new skills than for adults?

Sometimes, but not always. Children may be more willing to have a go without worrying about looking silly. Adults often know exactly why they want to learn, which can help them stay focused. The bigger problem for adults may simply be finding time to practise around work and family.

What is the most important thing for learning a language well?

I'd say using it regularly. You can know lots of words and still freeze when someone asks you a simple question. Speaking or writing for a real purpose helps turn that knowledge into something you can use. Getting feedback matters too, otherwise you might keep making the same mistake without noticing.

T05 历史知识与人才

观点方向： 宏大事件之外讲普通人生活；兴趣提供入口；多种才能满足不同需要，不把成绩等同所有能力。

表达： make the subject accessible＝让学科容易理解；bring the past to life＝让过去生动起来；different strengths＝不同长处

Besides big historical events, what else is important when learning history?

I'd like to know about ordinary people's lives as well. What did they eat, how did they travel, and what did they use for money? Those details make the period easier to picture. They can also help explain bigger events, because a war or a change of government affects people's daily lives, not just the rulers.

Why are some teachers’ classes boring? Are there any solutions?

Sometimes there's too much information and not enough explanation of why it matters. If a history lesson is just names and dates, students can easily switch off. A story, a picture or a question can give them something to follow. The teacher still needs to teach the facts, but there's more than one way to present them.

Why does modern society need talents of all kinds?

Because most things need more than one kind of skill. Take making an app: someone has to build it, someone has to design it, and someone has to explain why people should use it. Being brilliant at one of those jobs doesn't mean you can do all the others. A team works better when people bring different strengths.

T06 科技AI与视频

观点方向： 工具有学习成本；清晰任务与人的核查仍重要；视频适合演示，文字适合查找和细读。

表达： reduce unnecessary rework＝减少返工；a learning curve＝学习过程中的适应成本；check the output＝核查结果

What are the advantages and disadvantages of AI?

It can save time, especially when you need a first draft or a few ideas. But it can also give you something wrong or completely miss what you wanted. I had that problem when I used it for bead designs. Breaking the task into smaller steps helped, so I think you still need to guide it and check the result.

What can teachers do to stop students from relying too much on AI?

I'd ask students to talk through their answer, not just hand it in. If they can't explain it, they probably haven't learned much from the task. Teachers could also look at drafts or do part of the work in class. That way, students have to take part in the thinking, even if they use AI for some help.

Which is more helpful, watching videos or reading books?

It depends what you're trying to learn. If I want to see how someone draws a face, a video is much easier to follow. If I want to understand a complicated idea, I may prefer a book because I can stop and reread a paragraph. So I wouldn't choose one format for everything.

T07 广告名人新闻与身份

观点方向： 吸引注意不等于购买；新闻兴趣随生活相关性变化；国家认同可以来自文化了解而非排斥差异。

表达： catch attention＝吸引注意；feel relatable＝让人有共鸣；renewed attention＝重新受到关注

Why are many advertisements endorsed by celebrities? How useful are they?

A celebrity can get people to notice an ad in the first place. Fans may watch it even if they weren't interested in the product. Whether that leads to a sale is another matter, though. If the product isn't useful or costs too much, I might remember the person and still buy nothing.

Do people prefer local or international news?

I think people follow whatever affects them most. A change to the local metro line could matter more to someone's day than a major story overseas. But if their job involves other countries, international news may be very relevant. Most people probably pay attention to a mix, depending on what's happening.

How can people develop their national identity?

Learning about the country's history and traditions can help, especially through things people enjoy. Food, films or a local festival can make the connection feel quite personal. I'd also encourage people to learn about other regions of their country. That helps them see that a shared identity can include lots of different ways of life.

T08 城市建筑住房与噪声

观点方向： 地标观赏价值与日常便利分开；住房看交通、隐私、空间；行为要考虑场所而非禁止一切声音。

表达： everyday convenience＝日常便利；rely on lifts＝依赖电梯；suit your lifestyle＝适合生活方式

Do you think all old buildings should be demolished?

No, definitely not. Some are part of a city's history, and once they're gone you can't really replace them. But an old building also needs to be safe and useful. If possible, I'd repair it or give it a new purpose, like turning an old industrial building into a gallery, rather than demolishing it straight away.

Is it better to live in an apartment than in a house?

For me, an apartment in a convenient location would be better. I don't want to spend a huge amount of time getting around or looking after a large house. But a family that needs more space might feel differently. I'd look at the location, privacy and daily routine before deciding which type of home is better.

Should children not be allowed to make noise under any circumstances?

No, that would be impossible. Children are going to make some noise when they're playing or excited. What matters is where they are. Shouting in a playground is very different from shouting in a library, so adults should help them learn the difference rather than expect silence all the time.

T09 旅行交通与时间

观点方向： 计划减少浪费但不排满；旅行长度因目标而异；通勤同时集中造成压力；不要用个人体验推断所有人。

What is the ideal length for a holiday?

For me, long enough that I don't feel I'm rushing the whole time. A few days might be fine for somewhere nearby, but if the journey takes most of a day, I'd want to stay longer. I prefer seeing fewer places properly to ticking off lots of sights. Otherwise, I come back needing another holiday to recover.

What problems does it cause for transportation when everyone commutes at the same time?

Everything gets crowded at once. Trains fill up, roads get blocked and people can end up waiting much longer than usual. It can also be harder to book a ticket for the time you want. If schools and workplaces could spread their starting times out a little, that might take some pressure off.

Is it good to arrive early in any situation?

Not always. For a flight, yes, because missing it would cause a real problem. But turning up much too early at someone's home can be awkward if they're still getting ready. I think being on time means allowing enough time for the situation, rather than arriving as early as you possibly can.

T10 艺术电影阅读与创造力

Does art, like music or theater, have an impact on society?

Yes, it can get people talking about things they might not discuss otherwise. After a play, friends might talk about a character's choices and end up sharing their own experiences. Yes, to an extent. You can pick up things about family life, food or social habits from a film. But it's still one story, and sometimes the characters are stereotypes. I don't think they have to. Reading first lets you imagine the characters yourself, which can be nice. But sometimes a good film makes you want to read a book you'd never have picked up otherwise. T11 朋友家庭代际与庆祝

观点方向： 关系靠共同时间与可靠支持；不同观念不必冲突；节庆让分散的人团聚，食物承载记忆。

Why do people need friends?

Because it's nice to have someone you can be yourself with. Friends can listen when things go wrong, but they're also there for ordinary enjoyable moments. Even having someone to go for dinner with or talk to about a show makes life less lonely. It doesn't always have to be a deep conversation.

Why is there a generation gap between the young and the old?

They've grown up with different ideas about what a good life looks like. For older people, a stable job might have been the main goal, while younger people may care more about flexibility. That can lead to disagreements about work or marriage. Talking about why each person feels that way is usually more helpful than saying the other generation is wrong.

Why are there special foods on special occasions or events?

They help make the occasion feel different from an ordinary day. If your family always makes a certain dish for a festival, seeing it on the table can bring back memories. Preparing it together may be part of the celebration too. So it isn't necessarily about the food being expensive; it's about what people associate it with.

T12 健康运动与生活节奏

Why do some people like to watch sports events?

Some people love the sport itself, but others enjoy supporting a team with a crowd. I don't follow much sport, yet I enjoyed watching my university's basketball team play. When people around you are cheering, you get caught up in it. Not knowing the result also makes a live match exciting.

What effect does stress have on health?

It can make it harder to rest properly. If someone's still thinking about work when they get home, they may struggle to switch off and keep putting off things like exercise. Then the next day can feel even more tiring. People react differently, but I think having time to recover is important, rather than expecting everyone to cope with endless pressure.

Why do some people like to stay up late?

For some people, it's the first time all day that they can do what they want. They've worked, travelled home and done the housework, and now they finally want to watch a show or play a game. Going straight to bed feels like missing out. I understand that, even though staying up too late can make the next morning much harder.

T13 自然植物环境与规则

观点方向： 环境影响生活条件；方便的设施帮助遵守规则；规定需解释和一致执行；环保提议与法律事实分开。

I don't think we can choose one and ignore the other. People need jobs, but they also need clean air and water. For example, a factory may create work while causing pollution that makes the area unpleasant to live in. The aim should be to keep the benefits of development while reducing that damage, even if it costs more at first.

Why do some people like to grow plants?

For some people, it's relaxing to look after something and watch it grow. Others like the practical side: if you grow spring onions, you can use them in your cooking. You don't have to be an expert either. A few plants that are easy to keep alive can make a home feel nicer without taking up much time.

How can parents and teachers help children understand and follow rules?

Explain what the rule is for and follow it themselves. If a parent tells a child not to shout in a library but then talks loudly on the phone, the message is confusing. A simple explanation, like 'People are trying to read,' makes more sense. Being consistent helps the child know what to expect.

T14 动物照顾与帮助他人

I understand it, because I have a cat myself. When an animal lives with you every day, it becomes part of your routine and you get very attached. Owners also need to think about neighbours, noise and shared spaces. So I'd focus on responsible care and suitable conditions, rather than saying cities are automatically the wrong place for pets.

Should children be taught to be kind to others?

Yes, and small everyday examples are probably the best way. They could help someone carry something or include a child who's being left out of a game. Adults should show that kind of behaviour too. I'd also teach children to ask what help is wanted, because doing something for someone isn't helpful if it makes them uncomfortable.
